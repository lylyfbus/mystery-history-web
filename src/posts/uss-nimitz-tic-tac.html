USS Nimitz Tic Tac Encounter: The Navy Pilot UFO Sighting That the Pentagon Couldn't Explain

It was November 14, 2004, about 100 miles southwest of San Diego, California. Two US Navy pilots were flying a training mission over the Pacific Ocean. They belonged to Strike Fighter Squadron VFA-41, called the Black Aces. Their jets were F/A-18F Super Hornets.

Then they got a new order. They were to fly to a spot where something strange had been showing up on radar. The Navy’s best radar system had been tracking it for two weeks.

What the pilots found that day is often called the best-documented and best-witnessed UFO case in American military history. The Department of Defense spent years looking into it in secret. Later it shared the evidence with the public and confirmed that the videos were real.

The lead pilot was Commander David Fravor. He had graduated from TOPGUN, the Navy’s famous school for fighter pilots. He was also the commanding officer of the Black Aces, which flew from the aircraft carrier USS Nimitz. His wingman was Lieutenant Commander Jim Slaight.

Both men were experienced fliers with thousands of hours in the air. That morning they took off from the Nimitz for a standard air defense exercise. A larger group of Navy ships was taking part. One of them was the USS Princeton, a cruiser with the most advanced air defense radar in the world. The pilots expected a routine mission. They got something else.

The story really began about two weeks earlier. Senior Chief Petty Officer Kevin Day was the radar operator on the Princeton. His radar was called the AN/SPY-1. It is the main tool of the Navy’s Aegis Combat System. One day he noticed contacts that did not match any known aircraft.

The objects flew at heights between 25,000 and 60,000 feet. They moved fairly slowly, at about 120 knots, and they came in groups of five to ten. They hovered, zigzagged, and changed height fast. The strangest thing was this. They could drop from 80,000 feet to sea level in seconds. Any known aircraft would be destroyed by that kind of move, and so would its crew.

Day was not an excitable amateur. He had years of experience with the SPY-1. He ran tests and looked for glitches. He asked other operators for their views. The contacts did not go away. For about two weeks he tracked them again and again and wrote down how they moved.

On November 14, the objects were seen coming down from high up to a spot near the ocean. Day asked the Princeton’s combat information center to pass the news to the Nimitz air wing. Fravor and Slaight were sent to check it out. A second two-seat F/A-18F, flown by Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich, also went along to help watch.

Fravor and Slaight flew down toward the spot the radar had given them. Fravor looked at the sea and saw something odd. A patch of water was churning and foaming, though the sea around it was calm. It was about the size of a Boeing 737 airplane, and nothing seemed to cause it.

About fifty feet above that water, an object hovered and drifted in an uneven way. Fravor had never seen anything like it. He was a TOPGUN graduate with thousands of flight hours. He knew almost every kind of aircraft and missile in the US arsenal, and many foreign ones too.

The object had no wings and no tail. It was about 40 feet long. Fravor later said it looked like a Tic Tac, the small white mint. It was smooth, white, and plain. It had no engines, no flight controls, no markings, no windows, and no exhaust.

It moved with a tumbling, wobbling motion along its long side. It looked as if it were bouncing on a cushion of air. Fravor began to fly down in a wide circle from about 20,000 feet to see it better. The Tic Tac began to rise. It copied his path across the circle, as if it knew he was coming.

What happened next has been called a “dogfight,” though it was a very one-sided one. Fravor cut across the circle to get closer. The Tic Tac sped up at once. It crossed his path and changed direction without slowing down. Fravor said this broke every rule of flight he understood.

Then it left at huge speed. Fravor thought it was far faster than any aircraft he had met. The whole meeting lasted about five minutes.

The strangest part came afterward. Fravor flew back to the Nimitz. The radar operators on the Princeton said the object had shown up again at the CAP point. That is a set spot where Navy aircraft wait, and it was about 60 miles away. They said it got there in seconds. It seemed to know where the spot was, and it had moved faster than any known aircraft or missile could.

After Fravor’s flight, another pilot, Lieutenant Chad Underwood, took off from the Nimitz. His F/A-18F carried a Forward-Looking Infrared pod, or FLIR. That is a camera that sees heat. Underwood got what Fravor had not: a video of the Tic Tac.

The FLIR video shows a white, wingless, capsule-shaped object in the air. It makes sudden sideways moves that seem to defy the rules of flight. It has no wings, no tail, and no trail of exhaust. At one point it shoots off the edge of the screen at great speed.

The video was recorded on November 14, 2004. It leaked to the internet in 2007, and people began sharing it widely in 2017. In April 2020, the Department of Defense released it officially. It came with two other Navy videos, called “Gimbal” and “Go Fast.” The Department said that the videos were real. It said Navy people had filmed them, and that what they showed was still unidentified.

The things Fravor reported, the Princeton tracked, and Underwood filmed are not just unusual. Some experts say they seem impossible by what we now know about flight and materials. A fall from 80,000 feet to sea level in seconds would make forces that destroy any known material.

The Tic Tac had no wings, no engines, and no way to lift or push itself. Yet it hovered, sped up, and turned with ease. The object that reappeared 60 miles away did so in seconds. That would mean a speed of several thousand miles per hour, with no sonic boom and no exhaust.

Some aerospace engineers point to four traits that keep showing up in many UAP reports. UAP means unidentified aerial phenomena, which is the new name for UFOs. The traits are instant acceleration, travel between air and water, no heat trail, and no normal flight controls. These engineers say such abilities are decades or even centuries beyond today’s technology.

The Nimitz case was not the only one. In 2015, Navy pilots from the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier group were off the East Coast of the United States. They reported several meetings with unidentified aerial phenomena. These produced two more videos, which the Pentagon later declassified.

The “Gimbal” video shows a spinning, saucer-shaped object moving against the wind. The “Go Fast” video shows a fast object skimming low over the ocean. On both recordings, you can hear the pilots’ amazement. These cases showed that the Nimitz event was part of a pattern near US Navy ships that had gone on for years.

For years after 2004, the story stayed mostly inside the military. The FLIR video leaked in 2007, but few people noticed. That changed in December 2017. Two reporters wrote about a secret Pentagon program called the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program, or AATIP. It had studied UAP cases, including the Nimitz one, since 2007.

AATIP had received $22 million from the Department of Defense. A former military intelligence officer named Luis Elizondo ran it. After the news came out, many official steps followed.

In June 2021, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence sent Congress a Preliminary Assessment on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena. It said UAP were a challenge to national security. It also said the government could not explain 143 of the 144 cases it had reviewed.

In 2022, the Department of Defense set up the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, or AARO. Its job is to bring UAP studies from all military branches into one place. In September 2023, NASA published the report of its Independent Study Team on UAP. The team found no evidence that UAP were from other worlds. But it asked for a careful, scientific way to study them.

In July 2023, Fravor and Dietrich spoke under oath to a House Oversight Subcommittee about unidentified anomalous phenomena. Fravor described the Tic Tac in detail. He said the object showed abilities “far beyond anything that we have.”

A congressman asked whether he thought the object was a threat to national security. Fravor answered: “It has been 19 years and we haven’t figured it out. That is a threat.” Dietrich had stayed quiet in public for years. Now she confirmed her own account of that day.

It was a first in American history. Military witnesses to a UFO encounter spoke under oath to Congress, on camera, for all to see.

This case is not a blurry shape in a dark lake, seen for a few seconds by people with no training. It has not been passed down and changed over decades. Many trained military officers saw it. The Navy’s best radar tracked it. A military camera filmed it in infrared. The Pentagon studied it in a secret program, and the Department of Defense said the videos were real.

Many people call it the most credible UFO case in history. Still, nobody has explained it. The Pentagon says the meeting happened. The video is real, the radar data is real, and the witnesses are real. But what was in the sky over the Pacific Ocean on November 14, 2004? That question is still open.
