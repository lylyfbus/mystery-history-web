USS Nimitz Tic Tac Encounter: The Navy Pilot UFO Sighting That the Pentagon Couldn't Explain

It was November 14, 2004, off the coast of Southern California. Navy pilots were flying a training mission over the Pacific Ocean. Their jets were F/A-18F Super Hornets. They flew from the aircraft carrier USS Nimitz.

Then they got a new order. They were to fly to a spot where something strange had been showing up on radar. Radar operators on a nearby ship had been seeing odd contacts for days.

What the pilots said they found that day is often called one of the best-known UFO cases in American military history. Years later, the Department of Defense released three Navy videos. It said the videos were real Navy footage. It also said the things in them were still unidentified.

The lead pilot was Commander David Fravor. He commanded a Super Hornet squadron on the Nimitz. Another jet was flown by Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich. Each pilot had a weapons officer in the back seat. Together they said that four people saw the object.

That morning they took off from the Nimitz for a standard air defense exercise. A larger group of Navy ships was taking part. One of them was the USS Princeton, a cruiser with advanced air defense radar. The pilots expected a routine mission. They got something else.

The story began some days earlier. Fravor has said that the Princeton’s radar had tracked unusual contacts for about two weeks. Other crew members said it was about a week. The objects seemed to appear at about 80,000 feet. They dropped fast and then stopped at about 20,000 feet.

If those radar tracks were right, no known aircraft could do that. But radar can make mistakes. Experts have not agreed on what the radar showed.

On November 14, the radar showed the objects again. The Princeton passed the news to the Nimitz air wing. Fravor and Dietrich were sent to check it out.

Fravor flew down toward the spot the radar had given him. He looked at the sea and saw something odd. A patch of water was churning and foaming, though the sea around it was calm. Nothing seemed to cause it.

Above that water, an object hovered and drifted in an uneven way. Fravor said he had never seen anything like it. He was an experienced Navy pilot.

The object had no wings. Fravor said it was about 40 feet long. He later said it looked like a Tic Tac, the small white mint. It was smooth, white, and plain. He saw no markings and no exhaust.

It moved with a wobbling motion. Fravor began to fly down in a wide circle to see it better. The Tic Tac seemed to copy his path across the circle, as if it knew he was coming.

What happened next has been called a “dogfight,” though it was a very one-sided one. Fravor cut across the circle to get closer. The Tic Tac sped up at once. It moved so fast that it seemed to disappear.

Fravor said it was far faster than any aircraft he had met. He and the other crew said they watched it for about five minutes.

The strangest part came afterward. Fravor flew back to the Nimitz. The radar operators on the Princeton said the object had shown up again about 60 miles away. They said this happened less than a minute later.

After Fravor’s flight, another pilot, Lieutenant Commander Chad Underwood, took off from the Nimitz. His F/A-18F carried a Forward-Looking Infrared pod, or FLIR. That is a camera that sees heat. Underwood recorded the video of the Tic Tac. He is also credited with giving the object that nickname.

The FLIR video shows a white, oval object in the air. It has no wings and no tail that you can see. At one point it moves very quickly and leaves the view of the camera.

The video was recorded on November 14, 2004. It leaked to the internet in 2007. In December 2017, The New York Times published it, and people began sharing it widely. The Pentagon had said in 2019 that the videos were real. In April 2020, the Department of Defense released three of them officially. It came with two other Navy videos, called “Gimbal” and “Go Fast.” The Department said that Navy people had filmed them. It said that what they showed was still “unidentified.”

Fravor said the technology he saw was far better than anything the US had. That is a strong claim. A video cannot show size or distance well. The pilots had no way to measure how fast the object moved.

In 2023, he told Congress that the object seemed to know the jets were there. He said it copied his moves and then sped away.

Some skeptics have other ideas. A researcher named Mick West said that objects like these could be planes or balloons. He said that glare in an infrared camera can make them look strange. Other experts point to sensor problems. In 2023, the Pentagon released more clips. Some of them turned out to be ordinary airliners.

The Nimitz case was not the only one. In 2014 and 2015, Navy pilots from the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier group were off the East Coast of the United States. They reported several meetings with objects they could not identify. These produced two more videos, which the Pentagon later released.

The “Gimbal” video shows an object that seems to spin. The “Go Fast” video shows an object that seems to move fast over the ocean. On the recordings, you can hear the pilots’ excitement. These cases show that Navy crews kept reporting strange objects.

For years after 2004, the story stayed mostly inside the military. The FLIR video leaked in 2007, but few people noticed. That changed in December 2017. Reporters for The New York Times wrote about a secret Pentagon program called the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program, or AATIP. It began in 2007. The Pentagon says it ended in 2012.

AATIP had about $22 million over five years from the Department of Defense. A man named Luis Elizondo said he ran it. The Pentagon later said that he had no part in running it. After the news came out, many official steps followed.

In June 2021, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence sent Congress a Preliminary Assessment on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena. It looked at 144 reports from US government sources, from 2004 to 2021. It could explain only one of them, which was a large, deflating balloon. It said UAP clearly pose a safety of flight problem. It said they may pose a challenge to national security. The report did not say what the objects were. It listed possible groups, such as clutter in the air, natural weather effects, secret US projects, and foreign systems.

In September 2023, NASA published the report of its Independent Study Team on UAP. UAP means unidentified aerial phenomena, which is the new name for UFOs. The report gave advice on how to study such events with better data.

In July 2023, Fravor spoke under oath to a House Oversight subcommittee about unidentified anomalous phenomena. Another former Navy pilot, Ryan Graves, also spoke about his own sightings. Fravor described the Tic Tac in detail. He said the object went well beyond the science and tools that the Navy had at the time.

This case is not a blurry shape in a dark lake, seen for a few seconds by people with no training. Several trained military officers saw it. Radar operators reported tracking it. A military camera filmed an object in infrared. The Department of Defense said the videos were real Navy footage.

Some people call it one of the most credible UFO cases ever. Still, nobody has explained it. The videos are real, and the pilots are real. But the Pentagon has not said what the object was. What was in the sky over the Pacific Ocean on November 14, 2004? That question is still open.
