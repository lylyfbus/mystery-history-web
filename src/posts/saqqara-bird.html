The Saqqara Bird: Did Ancient Egyptians Understand Flight?

In the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, a small wooden object sits in a glass case. Around it are the golden treasures of pharaohs and the painted coffins of nobles. The little object is only 18 centimeters long, and it weighs just 39 grams. Someone carved it from one piece of sycamore wood more than 2,200 years ago.

At first, it looks like a simple model of a bird. But look more closely, and the Saqqara Bird becomes a puzzle.

Its wings are straight and set at a sharp angle. They are not curved like the wings of a bird in flight. Its body is smooth and narrows to a point. Its tail stands straight up, like the tail fin of a modern aircraft. A real bird’s tail feathers spread out flat.

It has no legs, no feathers, and no eyes. Some researchers say it looks strangely like a modern glider. A glider is an aircraft that flies without an engine.

The bird was found in 1898, when people dug out the Pa-di-Imen tomb at Saqqara. Saqqara is called the oldest necropolis in the world. A necropolis is a very large ancient burial ground. Most experts on ancient Egypt say the bird was a holy object, a child’s toy, or a weather vane.

But a small group of researchers feel strongly that it is much more. They say this carving proves that the ancient Egyptians understood how things fly. That would be many centuries before anyone was supposed to know.

Saqqara is one of the most important ancient sites in Egypt. The burial ground runs for miles along the west bank of the River Nile, south of Cairo. For more than 3,000 years, it was the cemetery of Memphis, the ancient capital.

The site holds the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2670 BCE. It is the oldest stone pyramid in Egypt. There are also dozens of other tombs, temples, and underground halls.

The Pa-di-Imen tomb comes from the Ptolemaic period, around 200 BCE. At that time, Greek kings ruled Egypt. They were the family of Ptolemy, a general of Alexander the Great. The tomb belonged to a man named Pa-di-Imen. His name means “gift of Amun.” The tomb held the usual kinds of objects that were buried with the dead in that age.

The bird was written into the museum list as a simple bird figure, number 6347. Then it was put away in storage, and few people noticed it for more than sixty years.

The object is small enough to sit in the palm of your hand. It is made of sycamore wood. It is about 14.2 centimeters (5.6 inches) long, and its wings measure 18 centimeters (7.1 inches) from tip to tip. It weighs only 39.12 grams (1.38 ounces).

There is a hole in the underside. This shows that the bird was probably made to sit on a stick or pole. The wood has grown dark with age, and the surface shows the wear of thousands of years. But the carving is exact and careful. It looks like the work of a skilled craftsman.

That changed in the late 1960s. Dr. Khalil Messiha was an Egyptian doctor. He studied the ancient world as a hobby, and he loved the science of flight. He visited the Egyptian Museum in Cairo and looked closely at the object. What he saw convinced him that this was no ordinary bird. He believed it was a small model of a glider.

Messiha pointed to several things that made it different from other Egyptian birds. The wings are straight and angled downward.

The tail fin stands straight up from the body. Modern aircraft have a tail like that, but no known kind of bird does. The object has no legs, no feathers, no eyes, and nothing else that belongs to a real bird.

He went further. He said the measurements of the bird matched the rules of modern aircraft design. He pointed to the length of the body compared with the wings. He pointed to its balance point and its upright tail fin. His claims got a lot of attention, above all when books and films about ancient mysteries showed the Saqqara Bird.

In 1991, a British researcher named Martin Gregorie studied the Saqqara Bird in detail. He built several copies and tested them as gliders. His results left no doubt. The Saqqara Bird, in its first form, cannot fly.

A glider needs a small flat wing at the back, called a tailplane, to keep it steady. The Saqqara Bird has none at all. Without it, the model is not steady in the air. It would tip forward and crash at once.

Gregorie then added a modern tailplane, which the ancient carving does not have. Only then did the model fly in a steady way. Even so, it flew badly next to gliders that were built to fly.

In 2023, researchers at the Institute of Aerospace Technology Bremen made the most careful scientific study of the bird so far. First they made an exact 3D scan of the object. Then they used a method called computational fluid dynamics, or CFD. In CFD, a computer works out how air flows around a shape.

With these modern engineering tools, the team tested how the Saqqara Bird would behave in the air. It fully knocked down the idea of an ancient aircraft.

The study found several deep problems with the claim that the bird is a working aircraft. First, its best glide ratio was extremely low.

There was a worse problem. The object’s center of mass, its balance point, lies near the back edge of the wing. The bird could not keep its nose level. If you launched it from any height, it would dive nose first, out of control.

The computer test also showed that the lift was not the same on both wings. Uneven lift would make the bird roll over in flight, out of control.

The researchers gave their verdict. They wrote that their results are “not consistent with the suggestion that the Saqqara Bird demonstrates ancient knowledge of aerodynamics.”

If the Saqqara Bird is not a model aircraft, then what is it? Most experts accept one answer. They say it was a holy object for ceremonies or a gift to a god, in the shape of a falcon.

The falcon was the most sacred bird in Egyptian religion. It was the living form of Horus, one of the most important Egyptian gods. Horus was linked with kings, the sky, and protection from the gods. Falcon figures are some of the most common religious objects found in Egyptian tombs.

There is another strong idea. The bird may have been a weather vane or a decoration on the mast of a sacred boat. Such boats were used in religious festivals.

Wall carvings in the Temple of Khonsu at Karnak show this. They come from the late New Kingdom. They show ceremonial boats with bird-shaped decorations on top of their masts. The hole under the Saqqara Bird was made to fix it on a stick or pole, and that fits this use.

The Saqqara Bird is famous far outside the world of Egypt experts. This is mostly because of the Swiss writer Erich von Däniken. In 1968 he wrote a bestselling book called Chariots of the Gods?. It spread the “ancient astronaut” idea, which says that visitors from space came to Earth long ago.

Von Däniken used the Saqqara Bird as evidence. He said that beings from other worlds had visited ancient Egypt. He said they had shared advanced knowledge with early peoples, including the secrets of flight. In the decades since, the bird has appeared in a great many films and books about lost ancient technology.

But scientists have always pushed back against this story. No ancient Egyptian text describes flight. No tomb painting shows an aircraft. No other object suggests that the Egyptians knew the science of flying.

The Egyptians were master engineers. They built the pyramids. But there is no evidence that they ever tried to build flying machines.

The real history of human flight begins much later. Abbas ibn Firnas lived in the 800s in Andalusia, in what is now Spain. He was a scholar of many subjects. He is given credit for one of the first recorded attempts by a person to fly. In about 875 CE, he built a machine like a glider and jumped from a high place. It is said that he stayed in the air for several moments before he crashed.

The science of flight had to wait for Sir George Cayley. This English engineer is sometimes called the “father of aviation.” In the early 1800s, he named the four basic forces of flight. They are weight, lift, drag, and thrust.

The first controlled flight with an engine came in 1903. Orville and Wilbur Wright made it at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina.

The Saqqara Bird still catches the imagination, because it is so hard to put in one box. The truly interesting thing is not the claim about ancient flight. It is what the bird shows about the skill of ancient Egyptian craftsmen.

The object is carved from a single piece of sycamore wood, and the work is very exact. Its smooth form shows that its maker had a natural feeling for shapes that suit the air. He liked how they looked, even if he knew nothing of the science behind them.

The Saqqara Bird also teaches a useful lesson. Looking like something is not the same as being it. An ancient object may look like a modern machine. That does not mean it worked like one. Science has shown this again and again, from Gregorie’s test copies to the CFD study of 2023. The Saqqara Bird cannot fly.

Today the Saqqara Bird is still in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, with the number 6347. This small wooden carving has won a kind of endless fame that its maker could never have imagined. And one question stays open. What did the carver mean it to be?
