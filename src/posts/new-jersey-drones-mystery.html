New Jersey Drones: The Mystery in the Sky

Lights over Morris County

In mid-November 2024, people in New Jersey began to look up at the night sky. Some of them did not like what they saw. They said that strange machines were moving over their homes, with bright lights in white, red, and green. Some of the lights seemed to hover and shift.

Many witnesses thought the machines were drones. A drone is a small aircraft with no pilot on board. But these drones looked much bigger than the ones in shops. Some people said they were almost silent.

One date matters most. On November 13, 2024, a first unauthorized drone was confirmed over Picatinny Arsenal. This is a U.S. Army center for making weapons and explosives, in Morris County. The sky above it is restricted, which means that no one may fly there without permission.

Soon more people were looking up, and more people were reporting. How many reports were there?

Thousands of reports

The word spread fast. Within days, sightings came from Somerset County and other parts of New Jersey. In early December, the FBI asked people to report drones near the Raritan River. Similar reports came from New York, Connecticut, Pennsylvania, and Maryland.

By December 10, the FBI tip line had more than 3,000 reports. By December 16, federal officials had reviewed more than 5,000 reported sightings. That is a very large pile of stories to sort.

Officials said that the drones they spoke of were up to six feet across. They flew for six to seven hours a night. Most drones sold in stores cannot fly for that long. Mayor Mike Ghassali of Montvale later said that the machines were 15 to 20 feet long. That is far bigger than the official guess. Size is very hard to judge in the night sky.

Ten more incursions were reported over Picatinny through December 6. A spokesperson said that the suspected drones were not part of any military activity at the base. So who was flying them?

What the officials said

The FBI, the Department of Homeland Security, and the FAA began to investigate. The FAA is the Federal Aviation Administration, which controls the skies over the United States. On December 12, John Kirby of the White House National Security Council spoke at a press briefing. He said that many of the reported objects were aircraft with pilots, flying legally. He also said that there was no sign of a danger to the country or of a foreign plot.

On December 16, several agencies issued a joint statement. They said that they had “not identified anything anomalous.” They said the sightings did not show a risk to the public or to national security. The statement was meant to calm people.

But many people felt that this did not match what they saw. Governor Phil Murphy wrote to President Biden on December 13 to ask for more help. Senator Andy Kim joined local police on a night patrol on December 12. A sighting that night was later identified as a small plane. President-elect Donald Trump suggested that the military should shoot down unknown drones.

By now, guesses were flying faster than the drones.

The “mothership” claim

Then came the most dramatic claim of all. Representative Jeff Van Drew of New Jersey said that the drones were coming from an Iranian “mothership” off the East Coast.

It was a big and alarming claim. But the Pentagon denied it. It said that there were no Iranian ships off the coast. No evidence has been shown for the claim, so it is the weakest of the ideas.

Another report said that drones followed a Coast Guard boat. Two lawmakers made this claim. Later records tied that report to passenger jets landing at a New York airport.

So some of the strangest stories fell apart when people checked them. What about the rest?

Planes, stars, and hobby drones

The joint statement said that many sightings were lawful drones, flown by hobbyists, businesses, and police. It also named aircraft with pilots, and stars that people took for drones. Later records tied some reports to a Black Hawk helicopter, a small plane near a nuclear power plant, and passenger jets.

Experts pointed out something else. A video of “large drones” over a house in Maryland was shown to be the constellation Orion. Orion is a bright winter group of stars. Someone who does not know the night sky could mistake bright stars and planets, such as Venus, for the lights of a hovering aircraft.

Near airports, planes with their landing lights on could seem to hover or move slowly. And since April 2021, an FAA rule has let small drones fly at night if they have bright lights. So there may have been more night drones than in earlier years.

These answers explain a lot. But do they explain everything?

The sightings that stay open

Not quite. Some witnesses were police officers and others with training. They said that what they saw did not match any ordinary explanation. And some facts are hard to wave away.

The unauthorized drones over Picatinny were confirmed by law enforcement at the base. So at least some drones really flew over a sensitive military research base. The reports there came again and again over about three weeks.

One odd claim came in November 2025, in a news report marking one year since the sightings. Mayor Ghassali passed on a story from a local drone hobbyist whom he trusted. The hobbyist said that one of the large mystery drones took control of his own drone and landed it. The man did not come forward in public. He feared harm to his career. So the story cannot be checked.

The same report said that dozens of sightings were still unresolved a year after the first wave.

Yet there is one more part of the story that is not about the sky at all.

How a rumor grows

Social media made the whole thing bigger. People posted blurry night photos, shaky videos of far-off lights, and more and more far-fetched theories. Every new sighting, real or not, could reach a huge audience.

This made a loop. The attention caused by the reports led to more reports, and those led to more attention. Some experts spoke of social contagion. That is when worry spreads from person to person, and people read ordinary sights as proof of something mysterious.

But it would be too simple to call all the reports mass hysteria. Drones were confirmed over Picatinny, so some real drones were in the sky. This is true even if most reports had ordinary explanations.

Has this happened before? Yes, many times.

An old pattern in the sky

In 1946, about 2,000 sightings of “ghost rockets” were logged, mostly in Sweden and nearby lands. These were rocket-like objects that streaked across the sky after World War II. Swedish and U.S. governments, among others, investigated them. In 1997, many people in Arizona saw a huge V-shaped group of lights in the Phoenix Lights case. The Air National Guard said that some of the lights were flares dropped by training jets. Many witnesses reject that explanation.

The pattern is familiar. First comes a believable early report. Then the media and social media spread it, and a flood of new reports follows. Many are mistakes. A small group of sightings stays unexplained.

So what do we know about New Jersey? Officials said that many sightings were lawful drones, aircraft with pilots, and stars. They found no sign of a national security threat. What is still open is the rest. Drones were confirmed over Picatinny Arsenal on November 13, 2024. Some sightings were still unresolved a year later. Some witnesses insist that what they saw was not an ordinary aircraft. What exactly were those lights? We still do not know.
